# Sensory pause — content and evidence review (2026-11-04)

## Source skill

Linehan's DBT distress-tolerance materials describe self-soothing through vision, hearing, smell, taste, and touch. The teaching notes frame the purpose as helping a person tolerate distress without making the situation worse. The accompanying worksheet asks learners to record the practice and rate their experience before and after. See [Linehan, DBT Skills Training Manual, teaching notes](https://www.guilford.com/add/linehan7_old/lin-p-4teaching.pdf?t=1) and the [publisher's worksheets](https://www.guilford.com/add/linehan7_old/lin-p-worksheets.pdf?t=1).

## App adaptation

`TechniqueId.SensoryComfort` offers one safe, neutral or pleasant cue already in the person's surroundings, for about 30–60 seconds. It records distress before and after, permits no change or an increase, and invites a practical next step when the underlying situation needs action. It avoids requiring users to manufacture a smell, taste, sound, or touch and tells them to stop if attention to sensation feels unpleasant.

This is a brief product adaptation of a DBT skill. The cited materials do not establish that this exact one-minute version reduces distress or works for everyone. The catalog, companion copy, and recommendations therefore make no efficacy promise.
