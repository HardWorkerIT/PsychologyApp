# Distanced self-talk — content and evidence review (2026-11-04)

## What the evidence supports

Kross et al. tested non-first-person self-talk (using a person's own name or non-first-person pronouns) across seven studies with 585 participants. The paper reports stronger self-distancing and includes studies of socially stressful situations. This supports the self-distancing prompt as a plausible short skill; it does not validate this app's exact one-minute script or guarantee relief for an individual. [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24467424/).

Moser et al. studied third-person self-talk in laboratory emotion-regulation tasks involving aversive images and autobiographical memories. One experiment found a change in an ERP marker of emotional reactivity within the first second. A laboratory marker is not proof that this mobile exercise reduces a user's distress or treats a condition. [Scientific Reports paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-04047-3).

## App adaptation

`TechniqueId.Observer` is retained for saved sessions but now presents a four-step, roughly one-minute self-distanced self-talk exercise in all seven catalog languages. It asks the person to name a situation and feeling briefly, address themself by name or as “you,” identify what matters and choose a safe next step, then check their current tension without expecting it to change.

The deterministic chat may offer it as an alternative for mild anxiety linked to conflict, humiliation, or performance situations. It is not routed when body symptoms, high intensity, trauma, loss, or another person's emotion are recognized. The general anxiety route remains fact-checking and practical problem-solving. The catalog text explicitly says not to excuse harm or minimize danger.

## Limits and review

- The exact one-minute app adaptation and its Russian wording have not been clinically or experimentally evaluated.
- No relief, speed, or treatment outcome is promised; tension may stay the same or increase.
- A clinician should review the practice, its social-stress routing, and safety wording. Native speakers should review the six non-Russian catalog versions before release.
- Usability and outcome data are needed before making efficacy claims.
